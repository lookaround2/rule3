PART 15: TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS
AND COMING INTO FORCE

INFORMATION NOTE
What this Part is about: This Part describes what happens to legal
proceedings that are going on when the new rules come into effect. The
Part is intended to ensure a smooth transition from the former rules to
these rules.
Words and phrases used in this Part that have defined meanings in the
Appendix [Definitions] include: applicant; contingency fee agreement;
Court; enactment; existing proceeding; file; former rules; judgment; judicial
centre; order; party; rules

PART 15: TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS AND COMING INTO FORCE. R.15.1
Deﬁnitions
15.1 In this Part,
(a) “existing proceeding” means a court proceeding commenced but
not concluded under the former rules;
(b) “former rules” means the Alberta Rules of Court (AR 390/68) in effect
immediately before these rules come into force.

New Rules Apply to Existing Proceedings
15.2(1) Except as otherwise provided in an enactment, by this Part or by an order
under rule 15.6 [Resolution of difficulty or doubt], these rules apply to every
existing proceeding.
(2) Every order or judgment made under the former rules and everything done
in the course of an existing proceeding is to be considered to have been done
under these rules and has the same effect under these rules as it had under the
former rules.

Related Provisions
1.6 (changes to rules)

A. General Presumption
There is no separate presumption that new legislation will not interfere with pending litigation.¹
Amendments to procedural law are usually presumed to be retroactive, unlike most other
statutory amendments.²
Where most amendments to an Act are expressly prospective only, one
about interest silent as to time of application may be taken to be retroactive.³
There are many
examples of amendments which have been held so retroactive, plus a few held substantive and
not retroactive.⁴
But that presumption will be rebutted where otherwise the change in Rules would
create a Catch 22.⁵
Constitutional rights must be substantive, so constitutional amendments to legislation are
presumed not to be retroactive.⁶

Though a right to appeal is a substantive right presumed not affected by new statutory
amendments, that right vests only after all preconditions to appeal under the former law are met
(such as an appealable defeat), not when the suit is commenced. So if the statute is amended to
remove the right to appeal, before vesting, the right is gone, especially if one can get the right by
a new procedure (such as leave. )¹

the suit continued against other defendants.³
If an application was made and heard while the former Rules were in force, the delay of the
judge in deciding until after the new Rules came in should not make the new Rules (if different in
effect) apply.⁴
In 2020, R.10.20 was amended to require that an order to enter a review ofﬁcer’s decision as a

examination for discovery had been held under the old Rules. Surprise and lack of expectations
are not the same as injustice.⁸

Medovarski case.⁹

C. Costs Tariffs
The Court of Appeal discusses precisely how changes in costs tariffs are applied. If the party
ultimately successful gets costs only because of the Court of Appeal’s judgment, his right arises
when it speaks, and the tariff then in force applies.¹
If the appellate costs tariff changes after oral
argument, while the decision is under reserve, the Court of Appeal may choose to use the old
tariff.²
A new form of Schedule C was used for trial costs after the Court of Appeal reversed the
trial judge on liability. The defendant defended an arguable money suit, thus gambling that the
procedures would be different at the end of the suit. There were no special circumstances to
depart from the former transitional R.601.1.³
The court can of course adopt some other basis for
costs, such as the former Schedule C, given the discretions found in R.10.29.⁴
The defendant delayed the plaintiff, who had to apply several times to compel various kinds
of discovery. And the defendant delayed trial. That was speciﬁc reason justifying use of former
Schedule C for costs when the defendant won an appeal.⁵
When both parties delayed, the new
Schedule C was used.⁶
Dispute Resolution Requirements
15.3 Rule 4.16 [Dispute resolution processes] applies to an existing proceeding
unless, before this rule comes into effect, discoveries under the former rules in
the existing proceeding have been completed.
All active steps in a suit were taken before the new Rules came into force, but once the new
Rules came in, the parties acquired an obligation to either settle or jointly move the matter from
stage to stage. If one party thought the matter was not progressing fast enough it should have
proposed a litigation timetable.⁷

Resolution of Difﬁculty or Doubt
15.6 If there is doubt about the application or operation of these rules to an
existing proceeding or if any difﬁculty, injustice or impossibility arises as a
result of this Part, a party may apply to the Court for directions or an order, or
the Court may make an order, with respect to any matter it considers appropriate
in the circumstances, including:
(a) suspending the operation of any rule and substituting one or more
former rules, with or without modiﬁcation, for particular purposes or
proceedings or any aspect of them;
(b) modifying the application or operation of these rules in particular
circumstances or for particular purposes.

PART 15: TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS AND COMING INTO FORCE. R.15.7
Filing of Orders or Judgments
15.7 Where on the coming into force of these rules a person has not ﬁled an
order or judgment that the person was required to ﬁle under the former rules, the
person must ﬁle the order or judgment within
(a) one year from the date on which the order or judgment was
pronounced, or
(b) 3 months from the coming into force of these rules,
whichever occurs ﬁrst.

Related Provisions
9.5 (entry of orders)

Increased or Decreased Time Limits
15.8(1) Where under these rules a time limit is provided for doing anything, other
than the serving of a notice or other document in advance of some event, that is
longer than the time limit provided under the former rules, the time limit provided
in these rules prevails, despite that the action or proceeding to which the time
limit applies was commenced under the former rules.
(2) Where under these rules a time limit is provided for doing anything, other
than the serving of a notice or other document in advance of some event, that
is shorter than the time limit that was provided under the former rules, a person
who commenced the action or proceeding to which the time limit applied under
the former rules must comply with
(a) the time limit under the former rules, or
(b) the time limit under these rules, calculated from the date on which
these rules come into force,
whichever occurs ﬁrst.
(3) Service of a document or notice that was effected under the former rules
prior to the coming into force of these rules remains valid despite any change to
the relevant time limit imposed as a result of the coming into force of these rules.

Related Provisions
13.5 (extending time)

This Rule is about time limits in the Rules of Court. Nothing in this or any other Rule permits a
court to extend times in a statute; only a statute can permit that.¹

Time Limit Under These Rules
15.9(1) Subject to subrule (2), where these rules impose a time limit for doing
anything for which no time limit was provided for under the former rules, and on
the coming into force of these rules the thing has not yet been done, the time
limit under these rules applies to the doing of that thing and is calculated from
the date on which these rules come into force.
(2) Rule 8.7 (Confirmation of trial date) applies only to matters that are set down
for trial after the coming into force of these rules.

Related Provisions

Time Runs from Different Event
15.10 Where the time limit provided by these rules for doing anything runs from
a different event than the equivalent time limit under the former rules, and on the
coming into force of these rules the thing has not yet been done, the time limit
provided by these rules applies, calculated either
(a) from the event speciﬁed in these rules, or
(b) from the coming into force of these rules,
whichever occurs later.

Formal Offer to Settle
15.11 A formal offer to settle an existing proceeding that was made and neither
withdrawn nor accepted prior to the coming into force of these rules remains
open for acceptance in accordance with the former rules.
