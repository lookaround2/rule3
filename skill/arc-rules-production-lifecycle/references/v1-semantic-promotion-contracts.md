## 1. General rule

Before promotion, identify the Appendix denominator entered by the target label and the exact predicate for numerator inclusion. Promote only when the source supports the target's legal meaning and completion predicate.

## 2. Proposition

Promote a source proposition only when it has:

- stable unique identity;
- singleton source ownership;
- exact evidence support;
- source/canonical separation;
- explicit role qualification or a governed unresolved state;
- no conflicting canonical owner.

Do not mark a proposition as canonical merely because the language sounds general.

## 3. Deontic operator

Promote when:

- modality is explicit: mandatory, permissive, prohibitory, discretionary, conditional, or definitional;
- actor or governed subject is identifiable where the text supplies it;
- object/action is identifiable;
- operator is linked to the proposition or test it governs;
- exact evidence is attached.

A fully wired deontic object is preferable to an isolated operator.

## 4. Legal test

A core-qualified test generally needs:

- governing authority or provision;
- exact proof;
- governed elements;
- threshold or decision rule;
- burden allocation or proof standard when applicable;
- exceptions and factors when the source supplies them;
- explicit verified absence or non-applicability only where the schema supports that state.

Do not transform a checklist, commentary summary, or discretionary consideration into a complete legal test without the missing components.

## 5. Issue

Distinguish:

- `IssueTemplate`: a reusable question raised by a rule;
- `LegalIssue`: an issue in a matter or case;
- adjudicated issue: an issue connected to a decision or resolution.

Do not add rule templates to an adjudication denominator. Promote to an adjudicated issue only with case ownership and a deciding/resolving relationship.

## 6. Remedy gateway

A fully routable gateway generally needs:

- available remedy;
- triggering conditions;
- blocker or disqualifying condition when applicable;
- alternative route where the model requires it;
- exact rule or authority proof;
- governed status.

If the rule supplies only a condition, consequence, or discretion, keep it as a remedy condition or candidate rather than a complete gateway.

## 7. Rule version

A court-facing version requires more than quoted text. Require:

- official source identity;
- official source URL/publisher where applicable;
- exact source hash;
- provision identity;
- temporal classification;
- effective/in-force interval or certified operative date;
- version relationship to the canonical provision;
- current-law and filing-ready boundaries;
- no unresolved identity conflict.

Secondary commentary can support a source-attributed candidate but cannot certify current law.

## 8. Argument and evidence

Do not classify rule commentary spans as litigation evidence merely to move an evidence-wiring metric. Use argument/evidence production labels only when the graph object represents an actual argument, evidentiary item, or reasoning relation required by the target schema.
