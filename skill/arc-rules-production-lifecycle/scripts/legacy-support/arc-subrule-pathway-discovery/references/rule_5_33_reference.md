# Rule 5.33 Reference Module

Use when analyzing implied undertaking, confidentiality, cross-use, public-record exceptions, leave, regulator/police disclosure, breach, and admissibility separation.

## Mandatory First Questions

```text
What is the source material?
How was it obtained?
Was it obtained under Part 5?
Is the proposed use inside or outside the original action?
Is the issue filing, collateral use, admissibility, privilege, remedy, or public access?
```

## Authority Examples

- Juman v Doucette, 2008 SCC 8: root implied undertaking doctrine.
- Secure Energy Services Inc v CCS Corporation, 2014 ABQB 107: heavy burden; collateral regulatory use caution.
- L(P) v Alberta, 2012 ABQB 309: Alberta three-part test.
- Kang v MB, 2019 ABQB 246: superior public interest / privacy complaint pathway.
- Makis v Alberta Health Services, 2020 ABCA 168: scope limit; not all litigation information is Rule 5.33 material.
- Terrigno v Fox, 2023 ABKB 89: public-record trap; attaching Part 5 transcript to affidavit does not automatically defeat Rule 5.33.
- Dreco Energy Services Ltd v Wenzel, 2007 ABQB 635 / 2008 ABCA 36: public-record / affidavit cross-examination distinction and related-action use.
- Hall v Willcox, 2011 ABQB 78: Rule 5.33 codification and Part 5 vs affidavit cross-exam distinction.
- RT v Alberta, 2022 ABQB 376: use is not admissibility; Rule 6.11 gate remains.
- Iozzo v Weir, 2004 ABQB 259 / LSI Logic Corp v Logani, 2001 ABQB 710: factors for lifting undertaking.

## Core Warnings

```text
filing permission is not collateral-use permission
collateral-use permission is not admissibility
admissibility is not weight
public filing does not automatically defeat Rule 5.33
Rule 5.33 does not create evidence
Rule 5.33 relief does not waive privilege
police/regulator disclosure is high risk without court direction
```
